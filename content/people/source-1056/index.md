Wan Zhuo, Ph.D., is a core member of the Jiangsu University Key Laboratory of Intelligent Medical Image Computing and the Intelligent Medical Research Institute. He received his bachelor's and master's degrees in computer science from Xi'an University of Architecture and Technology and the University of Warwick, UK, respectively, and received his PhD in computer science from the School of Computer Science, University of Warwick, UK. Currently, as the first author (including co-authors), he has published two SCI papers (SCI Area 1) and accepted one (SCI Area 1) in international journals in related fields, including Neuroimage (two articles, IF=6.5), a top journal in the field of neuroimaging, and EBioMedicine (one article, IF=8.2), a comprehensive sub-journal of The Lancet.

The main research areas are: developing data analysis and machine learning methods and their application in neuroimaging big data, modeling and analyzing large-scale and high-dimensional neuroimaging data, and realizing predictive analysis of mental illness and personality models, thereby providing reference for early diagnosis and precise treatment of diseases. Mainly dedicated to 1) prediction models based on brain function, 2) whole-brain correlation analysis, 3) analysis of post-traumatic mental health and cognitive function.

For more information, please visit my personal homepage: http://faculty.nuist.edu.cn/wanzhuo/zh_CN/index.htm

Representative papers:

1. Zuo Wan, Rolls ET#, Cheng W, Feng J (2020) Sensation-seeking is related to functional connectivities of the medial orbitofrontal cort ex with the anterior cingulate cortex. Neuroimage, 215:116845. (Impact factor: 6.5; Chinese Academy of Sciences Division: District 1)

2. Rolls ET#, Zuo Wan #, Cheng W, Feng J (2022) Risk-taking in humans and the medial orbitofrontal cortex reward system. Neuroimage 249: 118893. (Co-first author, impact factor: 6.5; Chinese Academy of Sciences Division: District 1)

3. Zuo Wan #, Rolls ET#, Cheng W, Feng J (2022) Brain functional connectivities that mediate the association between childhood trauma events, and adult mental health and cognition. EBioMedicine. (Impact factor: 8.2; Chinese Academy of Sciences Division: Region 1)
